Real problems, real disagreement. Most models learn from consensus — I study where it breaks down.

About

I work on real-world medical imaging problems, where the ground truth is rarely as clean as benchmarks assume. My focus is disagreement in clinical practice: two experts labeling the same cell differently, and the same model behaving differently on two microscopes. I treat both as signals rather than noise, and build models that know not only what to predict, but also when they are uncertain and when to defer to human expertise.

Research

Disagreement in clinical AI

Clinical labels disagree in two ways.
I study both.

1

Annotator disagreement — on the same object

Majority voting hides the fact that experts saw the same image and came to different conclusions. I train on the full distribution of expert opinions, so models can identify contested cases and give them extra attention. Developed with a partner fertility clinic, the work spans the entire pipeline from data collection to clinical reader evaluation.

annotator disagreementvisual ambiguityclinical deployment
2

Domain shift — on the same task

A model trained on one microscope rarely survives the next one; the disagreement here is between acquisition conditions rather than between people. My current work explores label-free vessel imaging and asks whether uncertainty can signal when a model has left its familiar domain.

Application

Reproductive health

A clinically important area that receives relatively little attention in machine learning. Using topic modeling, I study the women's reproductive health literature to identify research trends and support large-scale biomedical knowledge discovery.
